\chapter{Analysis and Discussion} \label{sec:results}
This chapter presents the experimental results of the ATHENA pipeline across two datasets: the original underwater image captioning dataset by Li et al.~\cite{mainref} and the UWBench dataset~\cite{uwbench}. The image enhancement pipeline is applied to both datasets during training and evaluation. We first report the backbone and hyperparameter ablation results on the Li et al.\ dataset, then analyze the effect of the image enhancement pipeline across both datasets, with particular focus on the UWBench dataset where images are substantially more degraded, and compare ATHENA against frontier vision-language models on the UWBench dataset as a common zero-shot benchmark. Finally, we analyze computational efficiency and deployment feasibility.

Across both DoRA settings, preprocessing provides no meaningful improvement on the Li et al.\ dataset; the Prep=OFF configurations match or slightly outperform Prep=ON on every metric. This is consistent with the nature of that dataset: its images are relatively clear, with limited turbidity or color distortion, leaving little room for enhancement to contribute. The preprocessing pipeline is therefore not necessary when input images are already of adequate visual quality.

To assess the pipeline's contribution under realistic degraded conditions, we re-evaluated Qwen3.5 on the UWBench dataset~\cite{uwbench}, which features substantially more challenging images with greater occlusion, turbidity, color distortion, and variable illumination, with and without preprocessing across all LoRA rank and DoRA configurations. All scores are reported as percentages ($\times 100$). Table~\ref{tab:prep_summary} shows the best result per condition; full per-rank results are in Appendix~\ref{app:results}.

Enabling preprocessing consistently improves performance across both DoRA settings. With DoRA disabled, the best preprocessing-on configuration (LoRA32) gains 3.35 BLEU-1 and 6.34 CIDEr points over the best preprocessing-off configuration. The gap is substantially larger with DoRA enabled: preprocessing-on (LoRA8) leads by 13.28 BLEU-1 points and 20.75 CIDEr points over preprocessing-off (LoRA16). This amplified sensitivity suggests that weight decomposition makes the model more responsive to input quality, since the directional and magnitude updates of DoRA more closely mirror full fine-tuning and therefore depend more on clean, enhanced inputs. METEOR and SPICE follow the same trend across both DoRA settings.

Taken together with the marginal impact observed on the original dataset, these results support a key finding: \textbf{the image enhancement pipeline is not universally necessary, but becomes essential when input images are visually degraded}. In the original Li et al.\ dataset, where images are already clear, the model can extract sufficient visual features without enhancement. In the UWBench dataset, and by extension in real constrained underwater deployments where turbidity, occlusion, and color distortion are common, the pipeline provides a measurable performance benefit. Although DoRA=True exhibits greater sensitivity to preprocessing quality, the DoRA=False configuration with LoRA rank 32 and preprocessing enabled achieves superior performance on four out of six primary metrics on UWBench (B-4, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, and SPICE). The configuration of Qwen3.5 with preprocessing enabled, DoRA=False, and LoRA rank 32 is therefore adopted as the ATHENA deployment configuration. Figure~\ref{fig:prep_gain} illustrates this contrast directly: the top panel shows Preprocessing ON and OFF scores on the Li et al.\ dataset, where bars are nearly identical across all metrics; the bottom panel shows the same comparison on UWBench, where Preprocessing ON consistently and clearly outperforms Preprocessing OFF.

\section{Computational Efficiency and Deployment Analysis}

To validate the feasibility of ATHENA's deployment on resource-constrained AUV hardware, we evaluated the computational footprint and inference latency of all three backbones under 4-bit NF4 quantization using the \textit{bitsandbytes} library, deployed as a \textit{FastAPI} server. Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} summarizes the resource utilization.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Resource utilization under 4-bit NF4 quantization}
\label{tab:efficiency}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Parameters} & \textbf{VRAM (fp16)} & \textbf{VRAM (Quantized)} \\
\hline
SmolVLM  & 1,267,694,576 & 1.49 GB & 604.48 MB \\
Qwen3    & 1,238,994,944 & 1.50 GB & 590.80 MB \\
BLIP-2   & 2,025,179,136 & 2.20 GB & 965.68 MB \\
Qwen3.5  & 1,369,148,224 & 1.77 GB & 652.86 MB \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Although Qwen3.5 has a slightly larger parameter count (1.37B) than SmolVLM
(1.27B), its 4-bit quantized VRAM footprint of only 653 MB is comparable to
SmolVLM's 604 MB, and both are well within the constraints of standard edge
computing modules. The NVIDIA Jetson Orin series typically offers 8 GB of
unified memory, leaving substantial headroom for system overhead and local image
storage. BLIP-2, by contrast, requires 966 MB under 4-bit quantization, making
it the least efficient option despite its intermediate parameter count.
Critically, Qwen3.5 achieves an inference latency of approximately 30 seconds per
caption. The image preprocessing pipeline adds only 0.03 seconds per frame across all backbones.
These latencies are well within acceptable bounds for underwater acoustic
communication scenarios, where data transmission rates rather than onboard
processing are the primary bottleneck.
